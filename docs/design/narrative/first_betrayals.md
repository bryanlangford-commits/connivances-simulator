# First Betrayals
_Draft. Work in progress. Canon owned by Bryan._

## Falla

Falla suddenly found herself falling. Turbulent winds thrashed and whipped at her hair. The cold air was biting, thin and hard to catch. Streams of tears clung tight to her cheek as they rolled across her face and out to her ears. Nearly blind from the biting cold and blasting wind, she could just make out the crags of the Echec mountain beneath her and panic slipped in. Everything she had trained for, every portent she had learned to read, and it would end here on a cold mountain she had wanted only to admire from the saddle. Her mind raced with what to do and what had happened. She recounted her steps. She had definitely strapped the saddle on the great bird. She was certain that she had checked that the stirrups were strong and that the cantle was intact and mounted high. Just moments earlier she had felt the utter elation of riding a Minikowa and had wondered if anyone had ever done such a thing before, when her footing seemed to slip out from under her and the saddle went sideways. The Grace Master of Divination would certainly be most disappointed at her lacking a well needed attention to detail, that is if she would ever see him again. She quailed at the sight of the fast approaching forest below. How did I not see this coming?

Falla began to search her person for anything that might save her from her descent. She grabbed her side satchel in hysteria, desperately digging, but nothing. She glanced up at the falling saddle still trying to figure out a way to soften the imminent impact, but it was no good. Her eyes rolled back into her head in concession.

Then it came to her. The bird. The invocation of her best friend Laere. She knew in her heart that she and the bird had somehow bonded, but it was still wild and she had never been able to command the beast. She had seen Laere do so once, unwittingly, but it was enough to spark a hope. Falla recalled that Laere had whipped his arm about his waist during an argument and the Minikowa rose to the ready as if to follow him. It was the first time that Austis, the Minikowa, had ever responded in kind to a gesture.

She rolled in the air and looked high for Austis. She caught his stoic glare and raised her arm high and repeated the gesture the way she best knew how. She held her breath, waiting, hoping, but nothing. Over and over Falla thrashed her arm at her side, trying desperately to get the bird's attention, trying to do anything she could to communicate a command to him before her consciousness slipped into darkness. Her limp body fell slack and a carousel of memories filled her mind.

Then something else. A foresight. Tree branches pounding and pummeling against bones and body. Deeply torn flesh crashing against huge pine branches. Blow after blow, body tossed, mangled, twisted and upheaved before finally coming to a thunderous rest against a hard stony floor. Falla's eyes blasted open from her presage. As her vision began to refocus, she saw it. She saw that Austis was coming. Determined, the bird flew, tucking and rolling, speeding his way to his rider. The landscape below was imminent. Austis paced alongside Falla. He reached out his mighty wing while Falla's hand grabbed for any kind of hold, any kind of hope. The bird tucked in closer. Falla caught the Minikowa's talon, and relief filled her face. The bird unfurled his enormous wings. They filled with rushing torrents of air that jolted him back. Falla's hand slipped away. Her arm stretched out as far as it could reach and closed on nothing. The wind roared in her ears, and for one strange breath she thought of Laere. Then her body splashed into the treetops and she disappeared into the piney forest.

## Read

By the start of their first day of training, Falla and Laere had already been close. Falla was to be conscribed to the Augury order and Laere the Conjurers, but their kinship was formed long before. They were both from the village of Theipeann though they grew up in separate circles.

Falla was the daughter of a merchant and on the path to learning about the world of products and influence. Many was the time her father, Jaord, would take her to his shop and show her his wares. He would tell her about why mountaineers preferred crampons to hand held spikes and why posh clothiers preferred silk to wool. She fancied herself in all aspects of life through the lens of her father's customers. She would pretend she was rich or an adventurer or a cartographer or fisherman; there were no limits to her imagination and her father's wares provided her with the most interesting and intriguing props to bemuse her mind at play.

Laere was the third born child of an established family of scribes. Intellectuals, their values lay in their ability to make quality replications of texts and scrolls. They taught Laere to read and write at a young age and provided him opportunities to consume the wisdoms of the old texts, study ancient maps, learn runeworks, and follow political histories. He was, as such, an autodidact; a savant of an eclectic collection of the kingdom's knowledge ranging anywhere from farming practices to military strategies to rituals performed to energize the land. By and far though, Laere's favorite things to read, the ones that wonderfully captured his imagination, were the "phenomenal histories". These were stories of people doing extraordinary and unbelievable things. Things meant to quell boiling points in the socio political and anthropological environment that unpredictably led to legendary events like "The great portent" where seers were able to predict the collapse of the cliff fronts over the village of Ancil or "Food for the masses" an unexpected and accidental conjuration of a brood of hens that saved the Artist district from the brink of starvation.

While their childhoods were spent mostly apart, Falla and Laere found friendship in one another during their early adolescent years. Laere's father Kelvan was a long standing customer of "Better than Goods", Falla's father's shop in the mercantile quarter. Kelvan would occasionally bring Laere along with him when he needed to refresh the family's supply of parchment and inks, but the kids were never more than cordial with one another. Falla's outspoken frivolity confused and sometimes even scared Laere who was less good with people, preferring to seclude himself away in a room to work, than to socialize. They were an unlikely pair, but neither of them would forget the events of the day they came to call one another friend. It was the day that Kelvan doubled over at the waist and fell to the floor in the middle of Jaord's shop. The shock of it struck immense fear in Laere's heart.

"Help!" Laere cried out. "Help? Help!" Jaord rushed from across the room. He looked over Kelvan feeling his face and limbs looking for symptoms that may have caused his collapse. "Falla, I need you to get the vial of garlic from the stockroom!" In a flash, this all seemed very familiar to Falla. She remembered the present like it had been the past. Voices slowed and time seemed to stand still. She could feel her heart racing, but it held no match to the clip at which thoughts raged through her head. It was as though tens of ideas could be worked through to completion before a single breath's cycle concluded. She had heard her father's words, but something didn't sit right. What is this? she thought to herself. "Falla, now!" her father urged and time came back to normal. The sense of urgency made the air palpable. She ran to the back room. "Vial, Vial" Falla said aloud. Laere examined his father as he held his hand. He was sweating profusely, his hands were cold, and he was mumbling as saliva trickled out from his lips. "Father?" Laere cried out. Kelvan began to convulse on the floor. "Falla, bring the bottle, bring the bottle!" Jaord urged again. Laere looked more deeply at his father and for a moment he found his panic was overcome by his instinctual and natural curiosity and a sort of calm washed over him. That's when he noticed it. "It's a mannice bite" Laere deduced aloud as he made the discovery. "It's a mannice bite!" The bite of a mannice was a rare thing indeed, but very distinguishable if you knew what you were looking at. Laere had discovered a large lesion that had formed around a small puncture wound on his father's forearm. He recognized it as the spreading of a neurotoxic poison and reasoned that the mannice's poison, capable of killing a cow in a day's time, would be the only thing that could so abruptly change his father's disposition. If he was right, then he knew what followed.
